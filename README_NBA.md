# ASPN — NBA Dashboards (R pipeline n1.1, ported from WNBA v4.3 + Predictions p2.0)

One self-contained `nba_dashboards.html`, rebuilt nightly from sportsdataverse's
public NBA release files (the ones hoopR's `load_nba_*()` functions read):
team and player box scores, play-by-play, rosters, schedules and player impact.
No server and no API key. Only team logos, headshots and Google Fonts load from
the internet at view time, and each has a fallback.

Everything in the WNBA v4.3 dashboard is here (Game Recaps, Matchup Previews,
Games / Opponent / Game type filters everywhere, past seasons and careers, the
Predictions tab). This file lists what is different.

**Games vs Predictions.** The **Games** tab holds everything game-level: upcoming games
for the next 7 days with win chance, spread and total (by day); recent results with the
pre-game pick each build logged to `predictions_log.csv` and whether it was right; the
matchup predictor; and Players & Availability. The **Predictions** tab is season-level:
playoff odds, how the preseason ratings were built, and the model report card. In
preseason, Recent Results shows last season's final game days until this season's are in.

**King of the Court tab.** The day's leader in PRA (points + rebounds + assists) across every
game that day; ties share the crown. Past Kings (every game day, tap a day for its top 10),
Most Crowns (with longest run and best night), Biggest PRA Nights, and a Crown Race for the
next 7 days: each player's projected line from the game model, then 6,000 simulated nights
for his chance to finish on top. The spread (sd = 7.5 + 0.10 x projected PRA, projections
pulled 5% toward 17) was fitted on 2025-26 player-games; in that test the day's favourite won
25% of days against 26% predicted. Your In/Out calls on the Games tab carry over. The **Matchup** column shows how much each game moves a player's projection against a
league-average opponent at the same venue, split into opponent defence (D, via the team's
projected score), pace (P) and blowout risk (B, minutes). A 2021-22 to 2025-26 walk-forward
test found these inputs, at their current size, sharpen the king odds (log loss -0.018 +/- 0.007
vs no opponent info); position-specific matchup factors and a larger defence weight did not help.

**Player lines before a team's first game** use 95% of each player's minutes-when-playing
from last season, not a 240-minute split of the opening roster (which ran stars ~3 min and
bench players ~5 min short in a 2021-2025 test; average miss 5.0 vs 6.2 min). Team ratings
and playoff odds are unchanged: they keep the rotation the roster adjustment was fitted on.
During the season, a team that hasn't played yet keeps its opening-roster lines until it
does (`pl_fill_unplayed` in predict_nba.R), so opening week has lines for all 30 teams.

## Setup

1. Install R (4.3+). The script installs `dplyr`, `tidyr`, `jsonlite`, `purrr`, `nanoparquet`, `xml2` if missing
   (`mgcv` ships with R).
2. Put the R files, the template and the .bat in one folder; run `Rscript build_dashboards.R`.
3. First run: downloads the current season (~25 MB, mostly play-by-play), the team box
   scores 2005 on (~140 KB a season) and six seasons of player box scores (~600 KB each),
   then calibrates the prediction model once (n1.1: about 15-20 minutes, was ~60; cached for the season).
   The first n1.1 run also looks up ~2,500 player birthdates on ESPN's athlete API (once, cached).
   Past seasons from `HISTORY_FIRST_SEASON` (default 2016) build once each (~9 minutes a season).
4. Later runs: ~9-10 minutes (the current season's lineup reconstruction is most of it).

Environment switches: `NBA_BUILD_HISTORY=FALSE` (current season only),
`NBA_HISTORY_FIRST=2020`, `NBA_SKIP_PREDICT=1`, `NBA_N_SIMS=10000`, `NBA_PL_HIST=6`,
`NBA_SKIP_INJURIES=1`, `NBA_SB_SIMS=500` (season backtest), `NBA_BIO_MAX_FETCH=3000`.

## n1.1: what changed (October 2026 audit)

Every change below is tested by the same walk-forward / leave-one-season-out machinery as
before; where an option didn't help it is left off automatically and the report card says so.

**Speed.** The player layer rebuilt every player's history for every game day (quadratic in
days); `pl_walker()` walks each season once and returns identical snapshots (checked to 1e-9
in tests/). Past seasons and the best-setting backtest are loaded once per build (`pm_memo`),
the backtest loop is ~3x faster, and the season simulation is vectorized (whole regular season
per block of simulations; Play-In and best-of-7s for all simulations at once from exact series
probabilities). One-time calibration ~60 min -> ~15-20 min; 10,000 seasons ~15 s.

**Team model.** Home court is now a parameter of the fit with a point-in-time prior (the last
5 seasons with fans; n1.0 applied a 2017-26 constant of +2.21 to every season back to 2006,
when home teams were +3.0). Last season's prior fades within the season (half-life fitted;
n1.0's never decayed). Each game's spread combines game noise with that matchup's rating
uncertainty (wider in October). The context fit has a home intercept, the right "eliminated"
cutoff before the Play-In era (top 8, not 10), and first-game travel from home.

**Season simulation.** Players out keep coming back: the injury report's return date, or the
historical absence curve, sets their availability game by game (n1.0 kept anyone who had
missed 3 games out for the rest of the season and the playoffs). The schedule's unpublished
NBA Cup slots (2 a team until December) are played vs a league-average opponent so every team
reaches 82. A new **season-level backtest** replays 2016-2026 from four dates and scores win
totals and playoff odds; it chose how much rating uncertainty persists through a season (n1.0's
win-total ranges were too wide: the real total fell inside the "80%" range ~92% of the time)
and whether strength drifts. The browser re-simulation now draws strengths together (it drew
them independently), uses the same availability, drift and unscheduled games, and tools/smoke.py
checks it against R.

**Availability.** Fitted on who was available before tip-off (n1.0 used who actually played),
jointly with rest/travel, with the option of counting the prior's opening rotation in each
rating's "baked-in" minutes. Traded players count for their new team from the day the roster
release lists them. Held out, almost all of the lineup layer's value comes from knowing who is
available: without an injury report it is no better than rest/travel alone -- hence:

**Injury report (injuries_nba.R).** Each build downloads https://www.cbssports.com/nba/injuries/
once, maps players to ESPN ids, and appends to `nba_injuries/injury_log.csv` (the raw page is
kept too). "Out for the season" and "out until at least <date>" set availability and the
opening rotation the roster prior uses; "Game Time Decision" plays 60% of the time until the
archive has 150 such rows, then the build measures it (same for how fast players return after
their date). If CBS is unreachable the build reuses today's last capture, else falls back to
"hasn't played in 3 games". If CBS changes its page layout the build warns and keeps the raw page.

**Age curves.** Birthdates from the roster releases plus ESPN's athlete API; year-over-year
changes (2010 on, point in time) give curves for box value and per-minute rates. Aging last
season's numbers is offered to the player lines, the lineup values and the roster prior; each
uses it only where it tests better.

**Prediction log.** Append-only; each game is graded on the last pick made before tip-off. A
failed read never rewrites the file. The .bat also backs up the log and the injury archive.

## NBA port: what changed

### Season and game structure
- **Season label = the year the season ends** (2025-26 = 2026). From August on the build
  looks for next season first.
- **PRESEASON mode.** When next season's schedule is published but no box scores are
  (an October build before opening night), the page shows the completed season and the
  Predictions tab projects the coming one from the calibrated prior, the new rosters and the
  schedule. The first build after opening night switches over automatically.
- **48 minutes:** 720-second quarters, 240 team minutes, per-36 player box rates, per-48
  lineup and team rates, pace per 48.
- **Play-In** (ESPN `season_type` 5) is kept and treated as postseason on the page (tagged
  "PI"; single games, never a series). Careers count it with the playoffs.
- **NBA Cup final** is re-typed 7 from the schedule's game type `CC`: it counts for nothing
  (standings, regular-season stats), so it shows only under "All games" with a CUP tag.
  Records everywhere are regular-season records.
- **Conferences and divisions** (`NBA_DIV`), including New Orleans' East years (≤ 2004).

### Court and shots
- NBA line: 23'9" arc, 22' corners; corner three = a three at or below 8.75 ft above the
  rim (14 ft from the baseline). The 5 table zones are now exact unions of the 12 chart zones
  (paint = the lane rectangle, not a 14-ft radius).
- ESPN tags only *missed* end-of-quarter heaves as "Heave Jump Shot"; they get their own
  shot family for inspection but are never dropped (that would inflate 3P%).

### League constants are fitted, not typed
`fit_league_constants()` fits on the last 10 completed regular seasons (cached):
home court per 100 possessions (2020-21 excluded), the Pythagorean exponent, and the
logistic win-probability scale used by Wins Above Bubble. 2025-26 build: +2.21 pts a game
= 2.20 per 100, exponent 13.77, scale 17.3 (the WNBA page hard-coded 2.5, 14 and 11).

### Archetypes
Prototype-seeded k-means (k = 9) on 16 role features (usage, assist / turnover /
rebound / block / steal rates, FT rate, 3PA rate and corner share, rim / paint / mid
shares, assisted share, self-created share, height). Players with 500+ minutes shape
the clusters; 100+ get an archetype. Shown on a new Archetypes tab (role cards, player
table, lineup shapes, roster construction), on every player page, and as "Defense by
archetype" on team pages and the league defense tab.

### Volume
An NBA season is ~1,320 games and 640k play-by-play rows (4x the WNBA). Lineup
segments and shots are written as fixed-width base-64 text (`pack_game`), 5.9 MB instead
of ~20 MB, and decoded once in the browser into the same arrays as before. The page is
~12 MB and loads in ~3 s.

### Predictions (predict_nba.R)
- **Incremental walk-forward backtest:** the decayed normal equations are carried day to day
  (identical to refitting; checked to the last digit), so 21 seasons x 60 settings take 5
  minutes instead of hours.
- **NBA bracket:** conference standings with the NBA tiebreakers (simplified: two teams —
  head-to-head, division leader, division record, conference record, point differential;
  three or more — division leader first), the Play-In (7v8, 9v10, loser v winner), four
  best-of-7 rounds (2-2-1-1-1), Finals home court to the better record. The browser
  re-simulation mirrors it exactly.
- **Offseason roster adjustment:** the preseason prior adds each team's roster change,
  valued by the lineup model; its weight is fitted leave-one-season-out.
- **Context:** rest, back-to-backs, travel (NBA arenas + the 2020 bubble), and
  conference-based "locked" / "eliminated" flags.
- Player layer: 240 minutes, 42-minute cap, 13 rotation spots, per-48 values; the
  player-line and lineup fits replay the last `PL_HIST_SEASONS` (6) seasons.
